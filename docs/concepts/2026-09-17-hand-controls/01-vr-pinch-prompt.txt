Input images: Image 1 is the aircraft identity, sky, lighting and calm atmosphere reference. Image 2 is the ivory/deep-teal UI palette and simple four-choice menu reference. Preserve those identities, but change the screen composition into a convincing first-person immersive VR scene with a small world-space panel and hand interaction. Do NOT copy the flat browser toolbar or the screen-centered desktop dialog from either reference.

Scene: expansive clear blue sky with a few pale clouds, a low grassland horizon and a short distant row of stylized green trees. The sky and aircraft remain the main experience, occupying the upper 70 percent with generous quiet negative space. One white civil four-engine passenger airliner with deep teal vertical tail, slim dark windows and swept wings flies gracefully in the upper-right, approximately 23 percent of screen width. All four turbofan engines should be anatomically plausible, clean and recognizable. Broad gentle golden side light glints on the white fuselage and wing, with no starburst lens flare. Stable, peaceful, heavy airliner flight, not an aerobatic or military pose.

Hands and panel: first-person viewpoint, no visible headset, no other person. Only two small natural human hands and short wrists enter from the bottom edge, kept below the aircraft and never dominating the composition. The left hand is relaxed near lower-left. A compact warm-ivory rounded rectangle menu floats just ahead of that left hand in the lower-middle of the view, apparently held steady in world space at a comfortable reach. The panel is NOT attached as a giant wristwatch and is NOT a flat screen overlay. Show subtle perspective and thickness, gently tilted upward toward the user's eyes, with clear depth separation from the hand and sky. The right hand approaches from the lower-right and uses an ordinary relaxed thumb-and-index pinch to select one option: thumb pad and index fingertip nearly meet, the other three fingers remain comfortably curved. Normal five-finger anatomy, no exaggerated gesture, no pointing gun shape, no controller. A tiny quiet teal selection dot and a softly tinted option are enough; no laser beam or glowing effects.

UI: premium calm museum / airport wayfinding character. Warm ivory base, deep teal text, restrained pale sage cards, fine borders, spacious and readable. Small panel title メニュー. Four large accessible choices in a tidy 2 by 2 arrangement, using these short Japanese labels: 飛ばす, 音を聴く, 見え方, 案内. Pair them respectively with a simple airplane outline, speaker/sound icon, eye/layers icon, and question-circle/help icon. A small close symbol in one corner is acceptable. Avoid extra text, disclaimers, metrics, fabricated chat, tooltips or labels outside the panel. The overall menu should occupy roughly 30 percent of frame width and 24 percent of frame height; the hands are smaller visual accents around it.

Color palette: warm ivory #F4F3EC, deep teal #154F55, pale sage #DEEBE5, clear blue #9BC7DC and warm golden sunlight. Render the airliner as an attainable high-quality 3D web-app model, environment restrained and cinematic but peaceful. Hand shading should feel integrated with the 3D scene, subtly stylized realistic rather than creepy photoreal cutouts.

Constraints: ONE coherent landscape visual, not a collage. No bottom browser bar, no desktop chrome, no large fixed HUD, no sci-fi cockpit, no neon or rainbow glow, no huge wave rings, no extra aircraft, no extra fingers, no giant hand, no fighter jet, no text clutter. The defining idea is: a small, reachable spatial control panel below a beautiful shared sky, while ordinary hand pinching makes the menu feel natural.
